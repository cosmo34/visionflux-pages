Privacy Policy — Oculus

Last updated: June 6, 2026

1. Data controller

Oculus is published by Cécile Candau (Picaza). For any privacy-related questions or to exercise your rights, contact: contact@picaza.fr.

2. Summary

Oculus is a local macOS application for video capture, recording, editing, conversion, and live streaming. We do not sell your data, do not serve advertising, and do not track your activity for advertising purposes (no trackers, no third-party analytics).

As a rule, your videos, audio, files, and settings remain on your Mac in folders you choose. Oculus does not operate a server that automatically receives your recordings or activity.

Data may leave your device only when you initiate it: streaming to a platform, copying files elsewhere, voluntarily sending a diagnostic report, or purchasing a subscription managed by Apple.

3. Data processed

3.1 Video, audio, and screen

  • Capture sources: streams from devices you select (camera, USB/HDMI capture card, microphone, audio interfaces).
  • Screen capture: if you choose the “Screen” source, Oculus captures your Mac display via system APIs (ScreenCaptureKit) only for preview, recording, or streaming that you start.
  • Network camera: if you configure a stream URL (RTSP, RTMP, HTTP, etc.), the app connects to that address to display and process the stream. You initiate this connection; the URL is stored locally in your settings.
  • Local processing: image filters, audio effects, overlays, encoding, and conversion are performed on your Mac. Streams are not sent to Picaza.

3.2 Files and projects

  • Recordings: video files created in the destination folder you designate.
  • Automatic transfer: optional copy to a second folder you configure (always on your disk or a volume accessible from your Mac).
  • Editing and conversion: access to media files you import or select via macOS system dialogs.
  • Exported/imported settings: JSON configuration files you save or open voluntarily.

3.3 Application preferences

Oculus stores locally (including via UserDefaults in the app container):

  • selected sources and devices;
  • favorite folders (via macOS secure bookmarks);
  • image, audio, encoding, and interface settings;
  • RTMP streaming destinations you configure (name, server URL, credentials if entered);
  • interface language.

This data is not synchronized with our servers.

3.4 Live streaming

When you start a stream, Oculus sends video and audio to the platform or RTMP server you configured (YouTube, Twitch, personal server, etc.). This transmission is required by the service you chose and is governed by that third party’s privacy policy. Picaza does not intercept or record these streams on its own servers.

3.5 Diagnostic logs

For troubleshooting, Oculus writes technical text files on your Mac in the app container’s Application Support folder (HotfixDiagnosticLogs). These logs may include:

  • timestamps, app and system version;
  • capture, recording, streaming, and conversion status;
  • error messages and technical traces;
  • file and folder paths you use;
  • excerpts from system logs (OSLog) for the Oculus process.

Stream keys and RTMP passwords are redacted in diagnostic exports. Automatic snapshots may be written periodically and when the app quits. You can view this folder or export logs from the Help menu.

3.6 Crash reports

If the app stops unexpectedly, Oculus may on next launch offer to send a diagnostic report. Sending is optional: nothing is transmitted without your consent.

If you accept, the app prepares a file with logs from the affected session and opens your mail client addressed to contact@picaza.fr. You must confirm sending in Mail; Picaza does not receive reports automatically in the background.

3.7 Subscriptions (Mac App Store)

Purchases and subscriptions are processed by Apple via StoreKit. We receive only the information needed to enable associated features (subscription status on the device). We do not have access to your payment details. Payment data processing is governed by Apple’s privacy policy.

4. macOS permissions

Depending on features you use, Oculus may request:

  • Camera: preview and recording from video devices.
  • Microphone: audio recording from the mic or capture input when you enable it.
  • Screen Recording: only for the “Screen” source.
  • File access: via system Open/Save dialogs and folders you authorize; the app only accesses locations you designate.
  • Network (client): connections to network streams, RTMP streaming servers, and occasional download of technical components (e.g. embedded ffmpeg) when required by a feature you initiate.

6. Retention

  • Recordings and projects: kept until you delete them.
  • Settings: kept in the app container until reset or uninstallation.
  • Diagnostic logs: kept locally; older archives may be purged automatically.
  • Reports sent by email: kept by Picaza as long as needed for support, then deleted or anonymized.

7. Security

Oculus runs in the macOS App Sandbox. Network, device, and file access is limited to declared entitlements and choices you make in the system UI.

8. Tracking and advertising

Oculus does not practice advertising tracking and declares no data collection for tracking in its Apple privacy manifest (NSPrivacyTracking = false).

9. Children

Oculus is not specifically directed at children and does not knowingly collect personal data from children.

10. Changes to this policy

This policy may be updated to reflect app changes. The date at the top indicates the current version. Significant changes may be mentioned in App Store release notes.

11. Your rights

Depending on your country of residence (including GDPR in the European Union), you may request information about processed data, rectification or deletion when under our responsibility (e.g. a crash report you sent us), or exercise your rights by contacting contact@picaza.fr.

For data stored only on your Mac, you can delete it directly (files, settings, uninstalling the app).
